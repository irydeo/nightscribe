Processing
----------
Extracted to a 512 px circular crop, transparent background (one-off dev
script, run on 2026-08-27). No other third-party data in this asset.

check.svg — white tick glyph used inside checked QCheckBox indicators
(gui/theme.py). Original graphic drawn by the project, 2026-09-10; no
third-party data.

HADS-stars.csv — catalogue of 168 high-amplitude delta Scuti (HADS) stars,
from Patrick Wils' HADS monitoring programme (Vereniging Voor Sterrenkunde /
AAVSO-VSX technical team). Columns: Name, RA, DEC, Max, Min, Period_h.
Copied on 2026-09-11 from the observatory workspace
(/home/boreal/Develop/astronomy/ns-hads/HADS-stars.csv) for the HADS feature
(see docs/HADS.md and docs/adr/ADR-034-hads-stars.md). Reference for the
programme: BAV Hamburg 2016, https://www.bav-astro.eu/images/BAV_Tagungen/Hamburg_2016/04_HADS.pdf

hads-coverage/HADS-Project-YYYY.csv (2011-2026) — same star list plus monthly
observer-coverage columns from Wils' monitoring programme. Bundled as
reference data for a future "coverage gap" feature (not consumed at runtime in
v1). Science reference for the feature:
Kotysz, K. 2020, "Variability of HADS stars in TESS", PTA Proceedings 10,
180-182, https://pta.edu.pl/proc/v10p180

welcome_sky.svg: the night sky behind the Welcome hero (Interfaz 1.4,
2026-10-01): gradient sky, Milky Way, a seeded starfield with a few
diffraction spikes, two constellation joins, the rolling ground and the
observatory silhouette. Original graphic drawn by the project (generated
once with a fixed seed so the stars never change between runs); no
third-party data. The Moon is NOT in the file: gui/widgets/welcome_sky.py
paints tonight's real phase over it from core/ephem_minor.py, so the hero
can never contradict the almanac. The ground, the dome and the slit carry a
rim light; only linear/radial gradients and basic shapes are used, which is
what QtSvg actually renders (no filters, masks or patterns).

world_map.json: the coastlines and land borders the site picker paints
(Interfaz 1.5, 2026-10-02). Source: Natural Earth, 1:110m Cultural Vectors
and Physical Vectors (ne_110m_coastline and
ne_110m_admin_0_boundary_lines_land), https://www.naturalearthdata.com.
Natural Earth is in the **public domain**: no permission or attribution is
required, and none of its terms restrict commercial use. Credited here
because a map without its source is a rumour. The file is the two layers
flattened to [lon, lat, lon, lat, ...] runs with 2-decimal coordinates
(about 1 km, far finer than a click on a 700 px map), converted once with a
one-off dev script.

world_places.json: the 1251 cities used to NAME a point clicked on that
map. Source: Natural Earth 1:50m Populated Places
(ne_50m_populated_places_simple), same public-domain terms as above. Only
name, country, longitude and latitude are kept (49 KB), rounded to 3
decimals; the nearest city within 250 km gives the point its name, and
beyond that the coordinates are written out instead, so the app never
claims a city that is nowhere near.

appicon.svg, appicon-*.png, appicon.ico, appicon.icns: the NightScribe
application icon, "quill drawing a comet trail into a star" on a night-navy
tile. Original graphic drawn by the project, 2026-09-21; no third-party data.
The SVG master is the source of truth; the PNG/ICO/ICNS set is rendered from
it (QtSvg rasterisation with supersampled downscaling, one-off dev script).

ufe_*.svg (17 files): the Unified FITS Editor's top-bar glyphs (ADR-044 rev,
2026-09-25). Flat 16x16 line icons in white, drawn by the project for this
feature: load/export, north/scale/annot/boxes toggles (on and slashed-off
variants), solve, move marker, and the zoom presets (Fit, 50, 100, 200, 400).
No third-party data, no font shapes (the digits are hand-drawn paths, so the
rendering never depends on Qt's SVG text support).
